# Emergency Food Storage & Rations Management

In crisis situations, food inventory management prevents premature starvation, maintains physical strength, and boosts morale.

## 1. High-Priority Emergency Staples
- **Grains & Pulses**: Rice, lentils (dal), oats, wheat flour, dried beans. Store in airtight containers with oxygen absorbers. (Shelf life: 5-25 years).
- **Canned Goods**: Tuna, chicken, beans, vegetables, fruits, condensed milk. Inspect for bulging or rust.
- **Fats & Sugars**: Cooking oil, ghee, sugar, honey (never spoils), salt (essential mineral balance), peanut butter.
- **Specialty**: Baby formula, instant milk powder, oral rehydration salts (ORS), multivitamin supplements.

## 2. Ration Calculation Guidelines
- **Standard Emergency Requirement**: ~2,000 kcal/day per adult (~1,500 kcal/day for children).
- **Water Consumption for Food**: Prioritize foods requiring minimal water for preparation (e.g. ready-to-eat canned goods, dried fruits, energy bars) when water supply is restricted.

## 3. Power Outage Food Safety
- **Refrigerator**: Holds safe temperature for ~4 hours if unopened.
- **Freezer**: Holds safe temperature for ~48 hours if full (24 hours if half full).
- **Rule of Thumb**: "When in doubt, throw it out." Eat perishable dairy, meat, and fresh produce first before opening canned/dried reserves.

## 4. Cooking Without Power
- Use camping gas stoves, alcohol burners, or outdoor charcoal/wood fires.
- *CAUTION*: Never burn charcoal or operate gas stoves indoors without active ventilation due to Carbon Monoxide (CO) poisoning hazards.
