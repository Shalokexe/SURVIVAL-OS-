# Emergency First Aid, START Triage & CPR Protocols

> **Classification:** Mass Casualty Incident (MCI) & Life-Support Protocol  
> **Source Grounding:** American Heart Association (AHA), European Resuscitation Council (ERC), Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment (START) Guidelines

---

## 1. The START Triage Algorithm (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment)

In mass casualty emergencies, triage determines priority of care in under 60 seconds per casualty.

```text
               [ ALL CASUALTIES ]
                       │
             Can the casualty walk?
             ├── YES ──────────────────► [ GREEN: MINOR ] (Walking Wounded)
             └── NO
                  │
             Is the casualty breathing?
             ├── NO ──► Open Airway ──┬── STILL NO ──► [ BLACK: DECEASED / EXPECTANT ]
             │                        └── STARTS ────► [ RED: IMMEDIATE ]
             └── YES
                  │
             Breathing Rate > 30 / min?
             ├── YES ──────────────────► [ RED: IMMEDIATE ]
             └── NO (< 30 / min)
                  │
             Radial Pulse Present / Capillary Refill < 2s?
             ├── NO (Absent / > 2s) ──► [ RED: IMMEDIATE ] (Treat Shock / Bleeding)
             └── YES (< 2s)
                  │
             Can the casualty follow simple commands?
             ├── NO ───────────────────► [ RED: IMMEDIATE ]
             └── YES ──────────────────► [ YELLOW: DELAYED ]
```

### Triage Category Definitions:
- **RED (Immediate)**: Critical life-threatening injuries requiring immediate intervention within 1 hour (airway obstruction, severe hemorrhage, tension pneumothorax).
- **YELLOW (Delayed)**: Serious injuries requiring medical care within 2-4 hours, but not in immediate respiratory or circulatory collapse (compound fractures, deep lacerations).
- **GREEN (Minor)**: "Walking wounded." Minor cuts, sprains, bruises. Can assist with other casualties.
- **BLACK (Expectant / Deceased)**: Non-breathing after single airway maneuver or injuries incompatible with life.

## 2. Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) Standard

When an individual is unresponsive and not breathing normally:

### The 30:2 Cycle at 100–120 BPM
1. **Position**: Place casualty flat on a firm surface. Kneel beside their chest.
2. **Hand Placement**: Heel of one hand on the center of the chest (lower half of breastbone); interlock second hand on top.
3. **Compression Depth**: Compress at least **5 cm (2 inches)** deep, but no more than 6 cm.
4. **Cadence (BPM)**: Compress at **100 to 120 beats per minute** (use audio metronome). Allow full chest recoil between compressions.
5. **Cycle Ratio**:
   - **Compression-Only (Hands-Only CPR)**: Continuous uninterrupted compressions at 110 BPM.
   - **Standard CPR**: 30 rapid chest compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths (1 second each with visible chest rise).
6. **Switching Rescuers**: Swap compressors every 2 minutes (approx. 5 cycles of 30:2) to prevent rescuer fatigue and shallow compressions.

## 3. Severe Bleeding & Tourniquet Application

1. **Direct Pressure**: Apply firm, continuous direct pressure using clean gauze, cloth, or trauma dressing.
2. **Tourniquet Placement**: If arterial spurting or severe limb bleeding cannot be stopped with direct pressure:
   - Place commercial windlass tourniquet (CAT/SOFT-T) **5–7 cm (2–3 inches) above the wound** (never directly over a joint).
   - Tighten until bleeding stops completely and distal pulse disappears.
   - Lock windlass rod and write the **exact application time (e.g. `TK 14:35`)** on the casualty's forehead or tourniquet band.
   - **NEVER loosen or remove a tourniquet** once applied in an off-grid survival scenario without surgical capability.
