# Extreme Weather & Improvised Shelter Building

In high-stress survival scenarios, exposure to extreme heat, sub-zero cold, wet weather, or severe winds can cause hypothermia or heat stroke within 3 hours. Proper shelter construction and thermal management are top priorities alongside water.

## 1. Core Principles of Thermal Protection
- **Radiation**: Prevent body heat loss to open space or cold sky by creating a reflective/insulated barrier overhead.
- **Conduction**: Never sleep directly on cold ground. Build an elevated bed or ground pad using dry leaves, pine needles, cardboard, or foam mats (minimum 10-15 cm thickness).
- **Convection**: Block wind exposure. Position shelter entrances 90 degrees away from prevailing wind directions.
- **Evaporation**: Keep clothes dry. Sweat accelerates cooling in cold climates. Layer clothing and ventilate when working.

## 2. Improvised Shelter Construction Types

### A. A-Frame Debris Shelter (Sub-Zero Cold / Forest)
1. **Ridgepole**: Find a sturdy log (2.5 - 3 meters long). Anchor one end to a tree fork or sturdy tripod, roughly hip-height.
2. **Ribs**: Lean heavy branches along both sides at a 45-degree angle to create an A-frame wedge.
3. **Thatch / Insulation**: Cover the ribs with a thick layer (30-50 cm) of dry leaves, pine boughs, grass, or bark.
4. **Interior Ground Bed**: Pack the interior floor densely with dry foliage. The internal space should be just large enough for your body to trap body heat.

### B. Tarp / Poncho Lean-To (Rain & Quick Setup)
1. Tie a ridge line rope tautly between two trees 1.2 to 1.5 meters above ground using a Bowline and Taut-line hitch.
2. Drape waterproof tarp/poncho over line. Anchor the windward edge flush to ground with wooden stakes or heavy stones.
3. Pull the front corners forward and anchor with guy lines for drainage pitch.

### C. Snow Trench / Snow Cave (Blizzard Conditions)
1. Dig a trench in deep snow approximately 2 meters long and 1 meter wide down to firm snow layer.
2. Cover the top with sturdy branches, tarps, or snow blocks set in an arched shape.
3. Heap snow over the roof structure (20 cm depth) for thermal insulation.
4. Dig a cold air sump at the entrance (15 cm lower than sleeping shelf) so heavy cold air drains away from body.

## 3. Heatwave & Solar Protection Protocols
- **Double-Tarp Shade Fly**: Set one tarp 30 cm above a lower tarp. Airflow between tarps reduces radiant ground heat underneath by up to 10°C (18°F).
- **Soil Heat Sinks**: Dig down 30 cm into shaded soil to rest against cooler earth during peak heat hours (12:00 to 16:00).
- **Ventilation**: Keep shelter sides open to maximize cross-breezes while shading from direct solar radiation.

## 4. Urban Structural Survival & Inspection
- In earthquake or storm damaged buildings, avoid interior load-bearing wall collapse zones.
- Stay clear of overhead hazards, unstable chimneys, and broken glass.
- Maintain clear emergency exit routes and keep a whistle, flashlight, and sturdy gloves at your sleeping spot.
