# Essential Tactical Knots, Rigging & Mechanical Advantage

Rope work, tensioning, and knot tying are essential survival skills for shelter building, haulage, cargo transport, and emergency rescue operations.

## 1. Top 5 Essential Survival Knots

### A. Bowline ("King of Knots")
- **Purpose**: Creates a fixed, reliable loop at the end of a rope that will not slip or tighten under load, yet remains easy to untie.
- **Applications**: Tying around a tree/anchor point, harness attachment, hoist loops.
- **Method**: "The rabbit comes out of the hole, goes around the tree, and goes back down the hole."

### B. Taut-Line Hitch (Adjustable Tensioner)
- **Purpose**: A friction hitch that slides along a standing line to adjust tension, then locks tight under load.
- **Applications**: Tarp guy lines, tent stake guy wires, securing vehicle loads.
- **Method**: Wrap around anchor post, pass inside loop twice towards anchor, then cross over standing line and make one final hitch away from anchor.

### C. Clove Hitch (Binding Hitch)
- **Purpose**: Quickly binds a rope to a post, pole, or cylinder.
- **Applications**: Starting and ending timber lashings, securing shelter ridgepoles.
- **Method**: Form two loops in opposite directions, overlap them, and slide over the end of the post.

### D. Square Knot / Reef Knot (Joining Equal Lines)
- **Purpose**: Joins two lines of equal diameter under light tension.
- **Applications**: Tying bandages, bundling firewood, light package cordage.
- **Caution**: *Never* use for critical human load bearing or joining ropes of unequal thickness (use a Sheet Bend instead).
- **Rule**: "Right over left, left over right."

### E. Figure-Eight Loop (High-Strength Loop)
- **Purpose**: Strongest, safest fixed loop for heavy load loads; retains more rope breaking strength than a Bowline.
- **Applications**: Rigging anchors, winch lines, climbing line endpoints.

## 2. Improvised Lashings for Shelter & Frame Construction

### Square Lashing
- **Use**: Joining two poles intersecting at right angles (90 degrees).
- **Step**: Start with a Clove Hitch on vertical pole. Wrap tightly around both poles 3-4 times. Apply 2-3 tight "frapping" turns between poles to draw wraps together. Finish with Clove Hitch.

### Shear Lashing / A-Frame Lashing
- **Use**: Joining two parallel poles at their ends to form an A-frame or bipod legs.

## 3. Improvised 3:1 Mechanical Advantage (Trucker's Hitch)
When pulling heavy logs, clearing debris, or tensioning main ridge lines:
1. Tie a **Directional Figure-Eight** or **Slip Loop** 1-2 meters back from anchor point on the rope to act as a pulley loop.
2. Pass the working end around the anchor tree/post and back through your pulley loop.
3. Pulling on the working end yields a 3:1 mechanical advantage, tripling your pulling force.
4. Secure with two half-hitches around the tensioned line.
