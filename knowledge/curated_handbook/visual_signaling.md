# Visual Emergency Signaling & Ground-to-Air Protocol Guide 👁️🔦

## 1. Principles of Emergency Visual Communications

When telecommunications, cellular towers, and radio frequencies fail during a tactical emergency or search-and-rescue operation, visual optical signals are the primary long-range method to attract rescue aircraft, reconnaissance drones, or ground search teams.

Visual signaling relies on **Contrast**, **Rhythm**, and **Standardization**:
- **Contrast**: High visual difference against natural terrain (bright orange, reflective silver, dark shadows against snow).
- **Rhythm**: Repeating sequences of 3 (Rule of Three emergency signal) or standardized Morse keying (`... --- ...`).
- **Standardization**: Utilizing international standard symbols recognized by military, civil air patrol, and coast guard pilots.

---

## 2. Ground-to-Air Visual Signal Codes (ICAO / NATO Standard)

When signaling aircraft, construct large symbols on open ground using rocks, logs, stamped snow, fabric strips, or trench shadows. Symbols must be **at least 2.5 meters (8 feet)** long and clearly visible from altitude.

```
       V                X                N                Y                ->
  REQUIRE HELP    REQUIRE MEDICAL        NO              YES         PROCEEDING THIS
                    ASSISTANCE                                          DIRECTION
```

| Symbol | Standard Meaning | Tactical Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **V** | **Require Assistance** | General emergency; stranded vehicle, power loss, evacuation needed. |
| **X** | **Require Medical Assistance** | Critical injuries, traumatic wounds, immediate triage required. |
| **N** | **No / Negative** | Responding to air-dropped messages or pilot wing-wags. |
| **Y** | **Yes / Affirmative** | Confirming drop zone safety or status inquiry. |
| **->** (Arrow) | **Proceeding in This Direction** | Marking evacuation foot path when abandoning campsite. |
| **LL** | **All Is Well** | Signal to searchers that team is uninjured. |

---

## 3. Aircraft Response Signals

Search and rescue pilots confirm visual signal contact through specific aircraft maneuvers:

- **Daytime Acknowledgment**:
  - **Message Received & Understood**: Pilot rocks wings side-to-side (wing-wag) or flashes landing lights.
  - **Message NOT Understood**: Pilot flies aircraft in a full 360-degree right-hand circle.
- **Nighttime Acknowledgment**:
  - Pilot flashes landing lights or green flare.

## 4. Signal Mirrors (Heliographs)

A signal mirror flash can be spotted by high-altitude search aircraft up to **40+ miles (65 km)** away, even on hazy days.

### Aiming Technique (Two-Handed V-Sight):
1. Hold the mirror close to your eye using the retroreflective center sighting hole (or index/middle fingers forming a V).
2. Catch the sunlight reflection on your hand or nearby ground.
3. Align the bright spot on your hand with the target aircraft seen through the sight hole.
4. Sweep the bright spot across the aircraft's cockpit 3 times repeatedly.

## 5. Pyrotechnic Flares & Smoke Signals

| Type | Best Used | Maximum Range | Safety Precautions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Orange Smoke Canister** | Daytime, High Winds | 3–5 miles (Line of sight) | Place on bare rock or damp earth; extreme fire hazard in dry brush. |
| **Red Aerial Meteor Flare** | Nighttime, Over Tree Canopy | 10–25 miles | Fire at 80° angle (downwind). Never point directly at low-flying helicopters. |
| **Handheld Red Signal Flare** | Night / Fog | 2–5 miles | Hold downwind; slag will melt clothing and skin. |

## 6. Optical Morse Code & Flashlight Strobes

When transmitting optical light signals at night:
- **Emergency Distress Signal**: Transmit `... --- ...` (3 Short flashes, 3 Long flashes, 3 Short flashes).
- **International Flash Rule of Three**: 3 consecutive bursts of light spaced 1 second apart, followed by a 1-minute pause.

```

 1s  1s  1s    2s    2s    2s     1s  1s  1s
```
